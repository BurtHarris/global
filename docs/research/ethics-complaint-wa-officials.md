## NOTE TO COUNSEL (not part of any complaint)

1. **Process confirmed directly from the EEB's own materials[^eeb-1][^eeb-2]:**
   - The EEB enforces RCW ch. 42.52 against "statewide elected officers and
     all other officers and employees in the executive branch, boards and
     commissions, and institutions of higher education." The Governor, the
     Attorney General, and the Secretary of State are each statewide
     elected executive-branch officers and therefore squarely within the
     EEB's jurisdiction — this is a jurisdictional fit the Background
     Report had flagged as unconfirmed; **it is now confirmed.**
   - **A separate complaint form must be filed for each individual
     officer** — the form itself states this requirement. This document
     is therefore organized as three separate draft complaints, not one.
   - **Five-year limitations period**: "The issue(s) you are concerned
     with must have occurred within 5 years from the date of the alleged
     violation."
   - Complaints must be submitted on the official form by mail (PO Box
     40149, Olympia, WA 98504-0149) or email (`ethics@atg.wa.gov`), not in
     free-form letter format.
   - The complainant's name is optional, but anonymity forfeits any
     ability to be kept updated on the investigation or consulted about
     details. A complainant may also request that identifying information
     be kept exempt from public disclosure under RCW 42.56.240 by
     initialing the form.
   - **Remedy structure:** the EEB process does not award the complainant
     damages. Its outcome, if a violation is found, is an administrative
     determination/enforcement action against the official — this is
     consistent with, and confirms, the Background Report's prior
     statement that RCW ch. 42.52 "has no private right of action for
     damages."[^15]
   - A copy of the official form, as downloaded, is saved locally at
     [`sources/eeb-complaint-form.pdf`](./sources/eeb-complaint-form.pdf).[^eeb-2]
2. **The single largest gap, carried over from the Background Report and
   not yet closed:** no specific act, date, or document has been supplied
   showing that Governor Ferguson, Attorney General Brown, or Secretary of
   State Hobbs personally used their office for personal gain or partisan
   advantage, as opposed to performing ordinary official duties (e.g.,
   holding a press conference, communicating with DOJ about a federal
   demand for voter data, or administering elections). **Each draft
   complaint below says so explicitly, rather than inventing specificity
   that has not been supplied.** Filing any of these in their current
   state risks the EEB declining to investigate for lack of a plausible,
   specific allegation — a central subject for Mr. Kirk's professional
   judgment before anything is submitted.
3. **Officeholder identification:** Governor Bob Ferguson and Attorney
   General Nick Brown are each independently confirmed via the YouTube
   video already cited in the Background Report.[^16] The Secretary of
   State, Steve Hobbs, is not independently confirmed via his own
   office's website in this research pass (its pages were not
   machine-readable to the tools available); his officeholding is
   inferred from his consistent appearance as the named official-capacity
   defendant in Washington election-administration litigation (e.g.,
   *Washington State Alliance for Retired Americans v. Hobbs*, No.
   3:23-cv-06014 (W.D. Wash.)), which is the normal case-caption
   convention for suits against a sitting Secretary of State in that
   capacity.[^hobbs] **This should be confirmed directly against the
   Secretary of State's own website before filing.**

## Complaint 1 of 3 — Governor Bob Ferguson

Following the EEB form's own fields and numbered questions:[^eeb-2]

**Subject:** Bob Ferguson, Governor of Washington, Office of the Governor,
Olympia, WA.

**1. Type of violation(s) being reported:** Based on the user's stated
underlying concern — "officials using their office for personal gain,
including for partisan advantage" — the closest-fitting checkbox
categories on the official form are **"Use of persons, money, or property
for private gain"** (RCW 42.52.020, .070) and **"Use of public resources
for political campaigns"** (RCW 42.52.180).[^15] **Neither category has
yet been tied to a specific identified act by Governor Ferguson; this is
a provisional categorization based on the general theory supplied, not a
conclusion that either box is the correct one.**

**2. When and where did the violation(s) occur?** **(P) Not yet
supplied.** The only dated, confirmed event connecting Governor Ferguson
to this matter is a January 26, 2026 press conference alongside Attorney
General Brown, held at a location not specified in the available
metadata.[^16]

**3. Description of the violation(s) in detail:** **(U)/(P)** No specific
statement, act, or document by Governor Ferguson has been identified in
this research that would, on its own terms, describe personal or
partisan gain from public office. The January 26, 2026 press conference
is confirmed to exist and to be officially published by the Washington
Attorney General's channel, but its audio/video content has not been
transcribed or reviewed by any tool available to this research, so no
specific quoted statement can yet be attributed to Governor Ferguson for
purposes of this complaint.[^16] **Plaintiff/complainant must supply the
specific timestamp and quotation relied upon, or a different specific
act, date, and document, before this section can be completed.**

**4. Supporting documentation:** The video itself (citation at n.16);
no other document has been identified.

**5. Witnesses:** **(P)** None yet identified.

**6. How do you know about the information you are reporting?** **(P)**
To be completed once a specific act is identified (e.g., personal
observation, news reporting, public records request).

## Complaint 2 of 3 — Attorney General Nick Brown

**Subject:** Nick Brown, Attorney General of Washington, Office of the
Attorney General, Olympia, WA.

**1. Type of violation(s) being reported:** Same provisional
categorization as Complaint 1 — **"Use of persons, money, or property for
private gain"** and/or **"Use of public resources for political
campaigns"** — pending a specific identified act.[^15]

**2. When and where did the violation(s) occur?** **(C)** January 26,
2026 press conference (same event as Complaint 1).[^16] **(P)** No other
date or location has been supplied. The Background Report separately
documents that Washington was among 24 states sued by the U.S. Department
of Justice over voter-roll access as of January 28, 2026 — two days after
this press conference — a timing the Background Report already flagged as
"plausible but unconfirmed" as a connection, not established.[^18][^21-bg]

**3. Description of the violation(s) in detail:** **(U)/(P)** As with
Complaint 1, no specific statement by AG Brown at the press conference
has been transcribed or quoted. If the theory is instead that AG Brown's
*legal defense* of Washington's election procedures against the DOJ's
voter-roll demands (see Background Report Part 7) itself constitutes
"use of office for partisan advantage," **that theory would need to be
stated explicitly and would face a substantial difficulty**: performing
the Attorney General's ordinary statutory duty of representing the state
in litigation is not, without more, a "private gain" or improper "special
privilege" under RCW 42.52.020/.070 — those provisions target personal or
third-party benefit obtained *through* the office, not the performance of
the office's own lawful defensive functions. **This distinction should be
confronted directly, not elided, before this complaint is filed.**

**4. Supporting documentation:** The video (n.16); the Senate letter
documenting Washington's inclusion among the sued states (n.18).

**5. Witnesses:** **(P)** None yet identified.

**6. How do you know about the information you are reporting?** **(P)**
To be completed once a specific act is identified.

## Complaint 3 of 3 — Secretary of State Steve Hobbs

**Subject:** Steve Hobbs, Secretary of State of Washington, Office of the
Secretary of State, Olympia, WA (officeholding inferred, not directly
confirmed — see Note to Counsel ¶3 above).

**1. Type of violation(s) being reported:** Provisionally, **"Use of
persons, money, or property for private gain"** and/or **"Use of public
resources for political campaigns,"** pending a specific identified act;
given the Secretary of State's direct statutory role administering
elections, a vote-dilution theory (Background Report Part 5) might
instead be framed here as **"Activities incompatible with public
duties"** if a specific administrative act or omission is identified.[^15]

**2. When and where did the violation(s) occur?** **(P)** Not yet
supplied. No document or statement by Secretary Hobbs has been located or
cited anywhere in this research to date — he does not appear in the
Background Report at all prior to this document.

**3. Description of the violation(s) in detail:** **(P)** Entirely
unsupplied. **This is the weakest of the three draft complaints as a
matter of evidentiary support:** no act, statement, policy decision, or
document attributable to Secretary Hobbs has been identified anywhere in
this research. If the underlying theory is that Washington's general
election administration (ballot mail processing, voter-roll maintenance)
diluted Plaintiff's vote, that is a claim about a *system*, not yet about
a *specific act of this official* — and RCW ch. 42.52 requires the latter
(a specific officer's specific misuse of office), not a general policy
disagreement. **Before this complaint is drafted further, a specific
decision, statement, or document from Secretary Hobbs (or his office)
needs to be identified.**

**4. Supporting documentation:** **(P)** None yet identified.

**5. Witnesses:** **(P)** None yet identified.

**6. How do you know about the information you are reporting?** **(P)**
Not yet supplied.
