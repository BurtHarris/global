# Identity and positioning

DOGEtech.co is a technology company serving the Americas. Its work is grounded in modern software engineering, practical systems thinking, measurement, automation, data analysis, knowledge organization, and careful use of artificial intelligence.

The company is not defined by conventional offices. Its distributed model uses communications, cloud services, collaborative tools, and resilient working arrangements. Work may occur wherever conditions make it practical and effective.

The operating model should remain adaptable. Events such as the Colombia earthquake demonstrate that location, connectivity, safety, continuity, and available infrastructure can change quickly.

## Short positioning statement

> DOGEtech.co applies modern technology, sound engineering, and plain sense to practical problems in the public interest.

## Longer positioning statement

> DOGEtech.co is a distributed technology company with roots in the Pacific Northwest and Colombia, serving the Americas. We apply software engineering, artificial intelligence, automation, data analysis, measurement, knowledge organization, and systems thinking to practical problems in the public interest. We work from wherever people, circumstances, and modern infrastructure make the most sense.

# Operating principles

## Plain sense

Begin with a clear account of the actual problem. Avoid jargon when ordinary language is sufficient.

## Public interest

Evaluate technology by the benefits it produces for people, communities, and institutions.

## Evidence

Identify sources and distinguish what was observed from what was inferred.

## Measurement

Define what improvement means and identify dimensions, units, baselines, and limits where applicable.

## Transparency

Make sources, assumptions, methods, and important limitations visible.

## Accountability

Preserve traceability among evidence, decisions, implementation, and results.

## Engineering discipline

Define requirements, constraints, interfaces, risks, dependencies, and measurable outcomes.

## Appropriate integration

Use technology where it improves a system. Technology is a means, not the objective.

## Human judgment

Use software and artificial intelligence to support responsible human judgment, not to conceal responsibility or treat generated output as authority.

# Editorial method

DOGEtech.co media should make evidentiary status visible. A recurring classification system can use the following terms:

## Fact

A statement supported by an identified source or a sufficiently established record.

## Observation

Something directly seen, measured, recorded, or officially reported, without adding a causal explanation.

## Evidence

Information relevant to supporting or challenging a proposition.

## Inference

A proposition derived from observations or evidence.

## Hypothesis

A possible explanation requiring testing against additional evidence.

## Conclusion

A determination supported by the stated evidence and method, with limitations made visible.

## Unknown

Information not presently available.

## Not yet verified

A claim that may be true but has not been adequately traced to reliable evidence.

## Compact sequence

```text
Observe → Measure → Understand → Improve
```

## Analytical sequence

```text
Facts → Evidence → Analysis → Conclusions
```

# Prime Air Flight 7598 video

This video follows the DOGEtech.co introduction. It will serve as an early demonstration of the company’s editorial and analytical method.

## Purpose

1. Counter misinformation arising during a fast-moving event.
2. Present official statements from Miami-Dade public-safety officials and the mayor.
3. Apply Burt Harris’s aviation-science background to explain relevant technical principles.
4. Separate established facts from observations, hypotheses, and matters still under investigation.
5. Avoid declaring a probable cause before the appropriate investigation produces evidence.

## Working editorial proposition

> A runway overrun is an observed event, not an identified cause. This report separates officially stated facts, direct observations, aviation-science analysis, and matters that remain unknown.

## Proposed structure

1. Opening and editorial proposition.
2. Primary-source Miami-Dade briefing clip.
3. Established facts and chronology.
4. What available images permit observers to say.
5. What a runway overrun is.
6. Landing energy and stopping-distance variables.
7. Rain, runway friction, and hydroplaning.
8. Evidence investigators will examine.
9. How misinformation develops during breaking news.
10. Preliminary conclusions and stated limits.

## Planned source clips

Burt will locate clips from YouTube or other accessible sources, with priority given to:

- Miami-Dade mayoral briefing.
- Miami-Dade public-safety briefing.
- Miami-Dade Fire Rescue statements.
- Miami-Dade Sheriff statements.
- FAA and NTSB statements when available.

For each clip, preserve:

- Source or broadcaster.
- Speaker name and title.
- Recording or broadcast date.
- Approximate timestamp.
- Original audio.
- Accurate captions.
- Sufficient surrounding context to avoid changing the speaker’s meaning.

## Proposed opening narration

> On September 6, 2026, Prime Air Flight 7598 overran a runway while landing at Miami International Airport. Images and official statements described an aircraft beyond the runway, vehicle impacts, fire, emergency response, deaths, and injuries.
>
> Images can help establish what occurred. They do not, by themselves, establish why it occurred.
>
> This DOGEtech.co report separates established facts, direct observations, possible contributing factors, and matters that cannot yet be determined.

## Aviation-science section

> A runway overrun is an outcome, not a cause. An aircraft must dissipate substantial kinetic energy during landing. Relevant variables can include aircraft weight, groundspeed, touchdown point, runway remaining, wind, runway condition, braking effectiveness, reverse thrust, aircraft condition, and crew actions.
>
> The physical relevance of a variable does not establish that it contributed to this accident. Investigators need recorded data and other evidence before reaching a reliable conclusion.

## Rain and hydroplaning section

> Rain can be relevant to a runway overrun, but the statement “it was raining” is not a causal analysis.
>
> Water can reduce tire-to-pavement friction. Contamination depth, pavement texture, drainage, tire pressure, tire condition, groundspeed, and braking demand may affect the result. Dynamic hydroplaning can sharply reduce braking and directional control, but wet-runway friction can also be reduced without full dynamic hydroplaning.
>
> Evidence of rain therefore supports a question: What braking conditions existed? It does not answer that question.

## Closing proposition

> We can explain the physical variables that ordinarily affect landing and stopping performance. We cannot assign those variables to Flight 7598 without the required evidence.
>
> That is not a gap to be filled with speculation. It is a boundary imposed by the available evidence.
